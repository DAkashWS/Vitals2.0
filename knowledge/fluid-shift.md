---
title: Fluid shift in space
source: Team research
---

## What is fluid shift in space?

On Earth, gravity pulls body fluids down toward the legs. In microgravity that downward pull disappears, so up to two litres of fluid, blood and lymph, shift upward from the lower body toward the chest and head. It starts within hours of reaching orbit and lasts the whole mission.

## How much fluid moves toward the head?

Up to about two litres of blood and lymph move from the legs and lower body toward the chest and head. That's roughly the same as a large bottle of water moving up your body.

## What is SANS, spaceflight-associated neuro-ocular syndrome?

SANS is an eye problem caused by the fluid shift. The extra fluid creates sustained high pressure inside the skull. That pressure pushes on the back of the eyes, flattening the eyeballs and swelling the optic nerves, and it can cause long-term blurry or degraded vision. Report any change in your vision to the flight surgeon.

## What is the puffy face, bird legs effect?

Fluid pooling in the upper body makes the face swell and the sinuses congest, like a permanent head cold, while the legs visibly shrink in volume. Astronauts call it "puffy face, bird legs".

## Can fluid shift cause blood clots in space?

Yes. The change in fluid pressure alters how blood flows through the big veins. On the International Space Station, NASA's Fluid Shifts study found that blood in the left internal jugular vein, in the neck, can slow down, stall or even flow backward. Stagnant blood sharply raises the risk of a dangerous blood clot. Neck swelling, pain or a new headache should go to the flight surgeon.

## What is orthostatic intolerance after spaceflight?

Orthostatic intolerance is dizziness or fainting when standing up after landing. In space the body senses the extra fluid in the upper body as too much blood, so it tells the kidneys to pass more water, and total blood volume drops. Back on Earth, gravity suddenly pulls the smaller amount of blood down to the feet, so not enough reaches the brain, causing severe dizziness or fainting on standing.

## Why do the kidneys get rid of water in space?

Sensors in the chest and neck feel the extra fluid that has moved up the body and read it as an excess of blood. The body answers by making the kidneys pass more water and by turning down thirst, so overall blood volume goes down. That's why astronauts can be short of water without feeling thirsty.

## What are Chibis suits, lower body negative pressure?

Chibis suits are special vacuum trousers. They use suction, called lower body negative pressure, to pull blood back down into the legs. That imitates Earth's gravity for a while and relieves the pressure in the head. They're also used before landing to help prepare the body for gravity again.

## What is fluid loading before landing?

Fluid loading means drinking a large amount of salt water, or water with electrolyte or salt tablets, shortly before re-entry. The salt helps the body hold on to the water, which quickly builds back blood volume and lowers the chance of fainting after landing.

## How does VITALS help with fluid shift?

Because fluid shift turns down thirst, you can't trust feeling thirsty in space. VITALS tracks the water you log each day against the 2.5 to 3.5 litre green range, so a quiet slide into dehydration shows up as a yellow or red flag before you feel it.
