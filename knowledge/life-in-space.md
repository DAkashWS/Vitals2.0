## What is the International Space Station?

The International Space Station (ISS) is a laboratory orbiting about 400 kilometres above Earth, travelling at about 28,000 kilometres per hour. It has been continuously crewed since November 2000. Astronauts from several countries live and work there, usually for about six months at a time.

## What is microgravity?

Astronauts in orbit aren't free of gravity: they're falling around Earth continuously, together with their spacecraft. That's why everything inside floats. This state is called microgravity or weightlessness.

## How do astronauts keep clean in space?

Water floats, so there are no showers on the ISS. Astronauts use wet wipes, no-rinse soap and no-rinse shampoo, and brush their teeth with edible toothpaste or spit into a towel. Keeping hands, skin and surfaces clean helps prevent infections and skin problems.

## Why do floating particles matter?

In microgravity, dust, crumbs, hair, liquid droplets and small objects don't fall to the floor. They float around the cabin and can get into eyes, the nose or equipment. That's why eye irritation is common and why spills are cleaned up with wipes straight away.

## How long does it take to reach the Moon and Mars?

A trip to the Moon takes about three days. A trip to Mars takes roughly six to nine months each way, depending on the planets' positions, and a full mission could last two to three years.
