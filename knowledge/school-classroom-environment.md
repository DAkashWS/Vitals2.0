---
title: Classroom environment
source: VITALS 2.0 school health guide (general guidance, reviewed by the team)
---

## What temperature should a classroom be?
Most classrooms are comfortable at roughly 18 to 25 °C. Below about 18 °C students get cold and restless; above about 26 °C they get sleepy, find it hard to concentrate and can get headaches. On hot days, open windows early while the air outside is cooler, close blinds against direct sun and use fans.

## What should we do in a classroom on a very hot day?
Let students drink water at their desks, keep blinds closed on sunny windows, use fans, and allow lighter uniform. Move PE indoors or to the coolest part of the day, give shade at break time, and watch for anyone who looks pale, dizzy or unwell, which can be heat exhaustion.

## What are the signs of heat exhaustion in a student?
Headache, dizziness, feeling sick, heavy sweating, cramps, being very thirsty, and pale, clammy skin. Move the student somewhere cool, let them lie down with legs raised, give water or a sports drink, and cool their skin. If they don't improve within 30 minutes, become confused, stop sweating or faint, call an ambulance: it may be heatstroke.

## Why does the classroom feel stuffy?
A full room of people breathing in a closed space raises carbon dioxide (CO2) and humidity, and stale air builds up. The fix is fresh air: open windows and doors between lessons, even for a few minutes in winter, and don't block air vents.

## What CO2 level is safe in a classroom?
CO2 is a sign of how stale the air is. Outdoor air is around 400 ppm. Below about 800 ppm the room is well ventilated; around 1,000 to 1,500 ppm students may feel sleepy and get headaches, so open a window; above 1,500 ppm the room needs airing straight away.

## Does stale air make students tired or give them headaches?
Yes. Poorly ventilated, high-CO2 classrooms are linked to tiredness, headaches, slower thinking and lower concentration, especially in the afternoon. Airing the room for a few minutes usually helps quickly.

## Does fresh air stop colds and flu spreading?
Good ventilation lowers the amount of virus floating in the air, so colds, flu and other airborne infections pass between students less easily. Opening windows, handwashing and staying home when ill work best together.

## What humidity is best for a classroom?
Aim for about 40 to 60 % relative humidity. Above about 60 % mould can grow and dust mites thrive; below about 30 % air is dry and causes dry eyes, sore throats, cracked lips and static shocks.

## What does mould in a classroom do to students?
Mould releases spores that can trigger asthma, allergies, sneezing, coughing and wheezing. Look for black or green spots and musty smells around windows, ceilings and corners. Report it, fix leaks and condensation, and air the room.

## How can I stop eye strain from screens and the board?
Use the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet (6 m) away for 20 seconds. Reduce glare by angling screens away from windows, keep text large enough to read easily, and remember to blink. Sore eyes and headaches after reading can also mean a student needs glasses.

## How do I know if a student needs glasses?
Squinting, sitting close to the board, copying mistakes from the board, rubbing eyes, headaches after reading, or tilting the head to see. Short sight is very common in school-age children. Tell the parents and suggest an eye test.

## Can classroom noise affect learning?
Yes. Loud, echoing rooms make it hard to hear the teacher, tire students out, raise stress, and are especially hard for students with hearing problems or attention difficulties. Soft furnishings, carpets, closed doors to corridors and quiet signals help.

## What triggers asthma in a classroom?
Common triggers are dust, chalk dust, strong cleaning sprays and air fresheners, mould, pollen from open windows in spring, cold air, smoke, and exercise. Students with asthma should always have their reliever inhaler close by.

## Is it safe to keep a class pet?
Class pets can trigger allergies and asthma, and animals like reptiles can carry Salmonella. Check for allergies first, keep the cage clean, and make sure everyone washes their hands after touching the animal or its cage.
