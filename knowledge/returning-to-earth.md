title: Returning to Earth
source: NASA Human Research Program
---

## Why do astronauts feel faint after landing?

After living in weightlessness, the body has less blood volume and the blood vessels and heart are used to not fighting gravity. Back on Earth, blood pools in the legs when you stand, and you can feel dizzy or faint. This is called orthostatic intolerance. It usually improves over days.

## What do astronauts do before landing to feel better?

Before re-entry, crews often drink extra water with salt tablets ("fluid loading") to boost their blood volume, and wear compression garments on the legs and lower body to help blood return to the heart. After landing, they're helped out of the capsule and often carried or seated at first.

## How long does it take to recover after a long mission?

ISS crews follow a reconditioning programme of about 45 days with daily exercise to rebuild strength, balance and fitness. Balance and walking usually come back within days to weeks. Bone takes much longer: it can take years to rebuild, and some of the loss may never fully return.

## Why is walking hard after coming back from space?

The brain spent months ignoring the balance organs in the inner ear, which don't sense "down" in weightlessness. After landing, it has to relearn how to use them, so walking, turning corners and standing up can feel wobbly. Many astronauts also say that things feel heavy, even a pen or their own head.

## Do astronauts have more back problems after landing?

Yes. After long missions, astronauts have a higher risk of a slipped (herniated) disc in the months after returning to Earth, as the stretched spine is loaded by gravity again. Careful lifting, core-strength exercise and the reconditioning programme help.
