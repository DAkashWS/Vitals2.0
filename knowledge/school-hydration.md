---
title: Hydration at school
source: VITALS 2.0 school health guide (general guidance)
---

## How much water should I drink each day?
Most school-age children need about 6 to 8 glasses (roughly 1.5 to 2 litres) of fluid a day; teenagers often need more. They need extra on hot days and when doing sport. Water and milk are the best choices.

## Why does drinking water help at school?
Even mild dehydration, losing just 1 to 2 % of body water, can make students tired, irritable and less able to concentrate and remember. Drinking regularly through the day keeps attention and mood steadier.

## How do I know if I'm dehydrated?
Signs include feeling thirsty, dark yellow or strong-smelling urine, peeing less often, a dry mouth or lips, headache, tiredness and feeling dizzy or light-headed. Thirst comes late, so drink before you feel very thirsty.

## What colour should my pee be?
Pale straw or light yellow means you're drinking enough. Darker yellow means drink a glass of water now. Dark yellow, amber or brown means you're dehydrated: drink and tell an adult if it doesn't get lighter. Some vitamins and foods can change the colour too.

## Can not drinking enough cause headaches?
Yes. Dehydration is one of the most common simple causes of headaches during the school day, especially after break or PE. Drink a glass of water and rest for a few minutes. A headache that is severe, keeps coming back or comes with fever, stiff neck or vomiting needs a doctor.

## When should students drink during the school day?
Little and often: a drink with breakfast, sips from a water bottle during lessons, a drink at break and lunch, before and after PE, and when they get home. An empty bottle by the end of the day is a good sign.

## How much should I drink during sport?
Drink before sport, take sips every 15 to 20 minutes during long or hot sessions, and refill afterwards. For most school sport, water is enough; sports drinks are only needed for long, intense exercise.

## Are energy drinks OK or safe for students?
No. Energy drinks contain high levels of caffeine and sugar and are not suitable for children and teenagers. They can cause a racing heart, anxiety, poor sleep, stomach aches and tooth damage. Many schools ban them.

## Are fizzy drinks, squash and fruit juice OK?
Sugary fizzy drinks and squash damage teeth and add empty calories. Fruit juice has vitamins but also a lot of sugar, so keep it to one small glass (about 150 ml) a day with a meal. Water and plain milk are the best everyday drinks.

## What is heat exhaustion and how is it linked to water?
Heat exhaustion happens when the body overheats and loses too much water and salt through sweat, often during sport on hot days. Signs are heavy sweating, headache, dizziness, nausea, cramps and pale clammy skin. Cool down, lie down, and drink water or a sports drink. If there's no improvement in 30 minutes or the student is confused, call an ambulance.

## Why do some students avoid drinking at school?
Some avoid drinking so they don't have to use the school toilets, especially if they're dirty or lack privacy. This can cause headaches, constipation and urinary infections. Clean, stocked toilets and permission to go when needed help students drink properly.

## Is it dangerous to overdo it with water?
It's rare in healthy students, but drinking huge amounts very fast (for example in a challenge or during endurance events) can dilute the salt in the blood. Drink to stay comfortably hydrated, not to force down litres at once.

## Does milk count as a drink?
Yes. Plain milk is a good drink for students: it hydrates and provides calcium and protein for growing bones. Tea, soups and watery fruits like melon also add some fluid.
