---
title: Hygiene at school
source: VITALS 2.0 school health guide (general guidance)
---

## How do I wash my hands properly?
Wet your hands, add soap and rub every part for at least 20 seconds: palms, backs, between the fingers, thumbs, fingertips and wrists. Rinse and dry with a clean towel or paper towel. That's about as long as singing "Happy Birthday" twice.

## When should students wash their hands at school?
Before eating, after using the toilet, after break and PE, after sneezing, coughing or blowing your nose, after touching animals, and after cleaning or handling rubbish. Hand sanitiser helps when soap isn't available, but soap and water is best for dirty hands.

## How do germs spread in a classroom?
Mainly through hands, shared surfaces and the air. Students touch desks, door handles, keyboards, pens and taps, then their face. Coughs and sneezes spread droplets. Handwashing, wiping shared surfaces, airing rooms and staying home when ill all cut the spread.

## How should I cough or sneeze?
Into a tissue, then put the tissue straight in the bin and wash or sanitise your hands. No tissue? Cough or sneeze into your elbow, not your hands. "Catch it, bin it, kill it."

## How often should desks and shared things be cleaned?
Wipe desks, door handles, light switches, keyboards and shared devices at least daily, and more often during flu season or a stomach bug outbreak. Shared equipment like PE gear and art tools should be cleaned between groups.

## How can I keep my lunch safe?
Wash your hands before eating, keep lunch boxes clean, use an ice pack for food that needs to stay cold (meat, dairy, rice), and throw away leftovers rather than eating them later. Wipe the table after eating. Don't share food, especially with anyone who has a food allergy.

## How often should I brush my teeth?
Twice a day, for two minutes, with fluoride toothpaste: last thing at night and one other time. Spit, don't rinse, so the fluoride keeps working. Cut down on sugary snacks and drinks between meals. Tooth decay is one of the most common reasons children miss school.

## What should I do about body odour?
During puberty, sweat glands become more active. Shower or wash daily and after PE or sport, wear clean clothes and socks, and use deodorant or antiperspirant. Wash PE kit after every use.

## How often should PE kit be washed?
After every use. Damp, sweaty kit left in a bag grows bacteria and fungi, which cause smells and can lead to skin infections and athlete's foot. Take it home the same day.

## What should I do about head lice?
Head lice are common in schools and are not a sign of being dirty. Check hair with a fine-toothed detection comb. Treat with wet combing or a lice treatment from a pharmacy, and check family members. Children don't usually need to stay off school.

## What causes athlete's foot and how do I stop it?
A fungal infection that likes warm, damp feet, often caught in changing rooms. Dry between the toes after washing, change socks daily, wear flip-flops in shared showers, and use an antifungal cream from a pharmacy if it starts.

## What makes a school toilet clean and safe?
Soap, toilet paper, hand drying, working taps and flushes, and regular cleaning. Students should report toilets that are dirty or out of supplies. Students who avoid dirty toilets may stop drinking, which leads to headaches, constipation and infections.

## How can we stop a stomach bug spreading in class?
Students with vomiting or diarrhoea stay home until 48 hours after the last episode. Clean up sick quickly with gloves and disinfectant, wash hands with soap and water (sanitiser doesn't kill all stomach bugs), and wipe toilets, taps and door handles often.

## What should I do if I get a rash at school?
Tell a teacher or the school nurse. Many rashes are harmless, but a rash with fever, a rash that doesn't fade when a glass is pressed on it, or swelling of the face or lips needs urgent medical help. Don't share towels, hats or hairbrushes.
