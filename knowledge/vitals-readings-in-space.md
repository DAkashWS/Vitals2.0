---
title: Understanding your readings in space
source: VITALS team; NASA Human Research Program
---

## What does my heart rate tell me?

Heart rate is how many times your heart beats per minute. VITALS treats 60 to 100 beats per minute at rest as green and 50 to 110 as yellow. In weightlessness, the heart doesn't have to pump blood up to the head, so resting heart rate can be a little different from on Earth. A heart rate that's much higher than your own baseline at rest can be a sign of dehydration, fever, stress, poor sleep or illness.

## What does SpO2 mean?

SpO2 is the percentage of oxygen your red blood cells are carrying, measured with a clip on your finger. VITALS treats 95 to 100 percent as green and 92 to 100 percent as yellow. Below that, check the cabin oxygen and CO2 readings, recheck with warm hands, and contact the flight surgeon if it stays low or you're short of breath.

## What room temperature and humidity does VITALS look for?

VITALS reads the room's temperature and humidity from its DHT11 sensor. It treats 18 to 33 °C as green and 16 to 36 °C as the yellow watch range. For humidity, 30 to 60 % is green and 25 to 75 % is yellow. Warm or dry air makes you lose water faster and dries out your eyes, nose and skin, so drink on a schedule and check the CO2 and airflow too.

## Why does VITALS compare me with my own baseline?

Normal varies from person to person: one astronaut's resting heart rate might be 55, another's 75. VITALS records a personal baseline for each crew member and flags changes from it, even when a reading is still inside the general green range. A steady drift away from your own normal is often the earliest warning sign.
