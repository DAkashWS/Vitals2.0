title: Space radiation
source: NASA Human Research Program
---

## What is space radiation?

Space radiation is high-energy particles that travel through space. The main sources are galactic cosmic rays from outside the solar system, particles from the Sun during solar storms (solar particle events), and particles trapped in Earth's magnetic field. On Earth, the atmosphere and magnetic field block most of it.

## Why is radiation dangerous for astronauts?

Radiation can damage cells and DNA. Over time, this raises the risk of cancer, and it may also affect the nervous system, heart and eyes (cataracts). It's one of the biggest health risks for long missions to the Moon and Mars, where there's less protection than in low Earth orbit.

## How are astronauts protected from radiation?

Spacecraft shielding, careful mission planning and monitoring all help. Astronauts wear or carry dosimeters that measure their dose, and NASA sets career limits on how much radiation an astronaut may receive. During a solar storm, the crew can shelter in the most shielded part of the spacecraft.

## Why do I see flashes of light when my eyes are closed?

Many astronauts see brief flashes, streaks or specks of light, especially when trying to sleep with their eyes closed. They're caused by cosmic-ray particles passing through the eye's retina or the optic nerve. The flashes themselves are harmless and are a known effect of space radiation, but tell the flight surgeon if you notice lasting changes in your vision.

## What is the South Atlantic Anomaly?

It's an area above the South Atlantic Ocean and South America where Earth's inner radiation belt dips closer to the surface. The ISS passes through it several times a day, and radiation levels on board rise briefly each time. Sensitive electronics can glitch there, and astronauts' radiation monitors record the extra dose.

## Is radiation worse on the way to Mars?

Yes. In low Earth orbit, Earth's magnetic field still shields the ISS from much of the radiation. In deep space, on the way to the Moon or Mars, crews lose that protection and receive a higher dose every day, mostly from galactic cosmic rays, which are hard to block. Limiting mission length, shielding and shelters for solar storms are the main protections.

## What should I do during a solar storm warning?

Follow the mission's radiation procedure: move to the designated shelter area, usually the most shielded part of the spacecraft, and stay there until mission control or the commander says it's safe. Keep your dosimeter with you.
