---
title: Bones, posture and sport
source: VITALS 2.0 school health guide, drawing on WHO physical activity guidelines (general guidance)
---

## How much exercise do students need?
The WHO recommends that children and teenagers aged 5 to 17 do an average of 60 minutes a day of moderate to vigorous activity, mostly aerobic, plus muscle- and bone-strengthening activities at least 3 days a week. Less time sitting and in front of screens also helps.

## When are bones built?
Most bone is built during childhood and the teenage years. Around 90 % of adult bone mass is in place by about age 18 to 20. Bone built now protects against fractures and osteoporosis later in life.

## What makes bones stronger?
Impact and loading: running, jumping, skipping, dancing, ball games, gymnastics and climbing. Bone grows stronger where it is stressed. Calcium, vitamin D, protein and enough sleep give the body what it needs to build it.

## How much calcium does a teenager need?
Ages 9 to 18 need about 1,300 mg of calcium a day (US guidance; UK guidance is a little lower). A glass of milk has roughly 300 mg. Dairy, yoghurt, cheese, fortified plant milks, tofu, green leafy vegetables, almonds and tinned fish with bones are good sources.

## Why do bones need vitamin D?
Vitamin D helps the body absorb calcium. The skin makes it in daylight; it's also in oily fish, eggs and fortified foods. In winter or for students who are rarely outdoors, a doctor may suggest a supplement.

## How heavy should a school bag be?
A loaded school bag should weigh no more than about 10 to 15 % of the student's body weight. For a 40 kg student that's about 4 to 6 kg. Weigh it on bathroom scales and leave behind what isn't needed that day.

## How should a school bag be worn?
Use both padded straps, tightened so the bag sits close to the back and not below the waist. Pack the heaviest books closest to the spine. Carrying a heavy bag on one shoulder strains the back, neck and shoulders.

## What is good sitting posture at a desk?
Feet flat on the floor, back against the chair, shoulders relaxed, and the screen or book at a height where the head isn't tipped far forward. Getting up and moving every 30 to 45 minutes matters more than perfect posture.

## Why does my neck hurt after using my phone?
Looking down at a phone or tablet for long periods puts extra load on the neck ("tech neck"). Hold the device nearer eye level, take breaks, and roll the shoulders and stretch the neck gently.

## What are growth plates?
Growth plates are softer areas of cartilage near the ends of long bones where growth happens. They are weaker than the bone around them, so in children a hard fall or twist can injure the growth plate. Pain near a joint after an injury in a growing child should be checked by a doctor.

## What should I do for a sprain or strain?
Stop playing. Rest the injured part, apply a wrapped ice pack for up to 20 minutes every 2 to 3 hours, use a compression bandage if comfortable, and keep it raised. See a doctor or school nurse if the student can't put weight on it, it looks deformed, or it isn't improving after 2 days.

## How can I tell if a bone is broken?
Possible signs: severe pain, swelling, bruising, a strange shape or angle, a grinding feeling, or being unable to move or put weight on it. Keep the injured part still, support it, and get medical help. Call an ambulance if the bone is through the skin, the student is very pale or faint, or it's a neck, back or thigh injury.

## What should happen after a head knock in sport?
Any student who has a knock to the head and is dizzy, confused, sick, has a headache, blurred vision or seems "not right" must stop playing for the day and be checked by an adult. Call an ambulance if they were knocked out, are vomiting repeatedly, have a seizure, or get worse. When in doubt, sit them out.

## Why do my knees hurt when I'm growing?
Growing pains are common aching in the legs, usually in the evening or at night, that go away by morning. Knee pain just below the kneecap in active teenagers can be Osgood-Schlatter disease, which settles with rest. Pain with swelling, limping, fever or pain that wakes a student every night should be seen by a doctor.

## How can students prevent sports injuries?
Warm up before sport, build up training gradually, use the right shoes and protective gear, drink water, rest between hard sessions and don't play through pain. Strength and balance exercises help prevent ankle and knee injuries.
