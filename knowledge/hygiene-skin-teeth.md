---
title: Hygiene, skin, hair and teeth in space
source: NASA (living on the ISS)
---

## How do you wash your hair in space?

Astronauts use no-rinse shampoo. They squeeze a little water onto their hair, massage in the shampoo, and towel it dry, catching any loose drops. Air filters collect the moisture. Long hair is often tied back so it doesn't float into eyes or equipment.

## How do you brush your teeth in space?

Brush as usual with a little water, then either swallow the toothpaste (some is made to be edible) or spit into a towel or tissue. Dental care matters because there's no dentist on board: brush twice a day and floss.

## How do you use the toilet in space?

Space toilets use airflow instead of gravity: a fan pulls urine into a hose and solid waste into a container. On the ISS, urine is recycled into drinking water. Using the toilet correctly takes practice, and the crew trains on Earth first.

## Why is skin so dry and itchy in space?

The air on a spacecraft is kept dry and is constantly circulated, and astronauts can't shower. Skin rashes and irritation are among the most common health complaints in space. Use moisturiser, keep skin clean with no-rinse soap, and report a rash that spreads, blisters or comes with fever.

## How do you cut nails and hair in space?

Nail clippings and hair floating in the cabin can get into eyes, lungs and equipment. Astronauts trim nails near an air vent so clippings are pulled into the filter, and cut hair with clippers attached to a vacuum hose.

## How do astronauts wash their clothes?

They don't. There's no washing machine on the ISS, so clothes are worn for several days, exercise clothes for longer, and then thrown away with the rubbish. Clean clothes come up on supply ships.
