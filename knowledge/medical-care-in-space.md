---
title: Medical care in space
source: NASA (crew health and performance)
---

## Who is the flight surgeon?

The flight surgeon is a doctor on the ground who specialises in space medicine and looks after the crew's health before, during and after the mission. In flight, they review health data, talk privately with crew members, and guide treatment using the on-board medical kit.

## Who gives medical care on board?

Each crew includes one or more crew medical officers: astronauts trained to give first aid and basic medical care, such as giving injections, stitching wounds and using diagnostic tools like ultrasound, with guidance from the flight surgeon.

## What is in the medical kit?

Spacecraft carry a medical kit with first-aid supplies, medicines, diagnostic equipment and emergency equipment. What's inside depends on the mission. Medicines are used only as the flight surgeon directs.

## What happens if there's a medical emergency far from Earth?

Near Earth, a crew can talk to doctors in real time and, in a serious emergency, return home within hours to days. On a mission to Mars, messages can take up to about 20 minutes each way and return can take months, so the crew has to recognise and handle problems themselves. That's why VITALS works fully on board and flags changes early.

## How do astronauts check their health in space?

Crews do regular check-ups in flight: heart rate, blood pressure, eye exams, blood and urine samples, body mass measurements, fitness tests and private conversations with the flight surgeon. VITALS adds a simple daily log with green, yellow and red alerts, so changes are spotted early.
